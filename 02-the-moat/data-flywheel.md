# Data Flywheel Map

> Score each loop 1-5. Your weakest loop is where competitors attack first.

## Flywheel Loops

| Loop                       | What It Measures                                                                   | Score 1          | Score 5                               | Score |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------- | ----- |
| **Correction**             | Does governance feedback improve future recommendations?                           | No learning      | Recommendations improve automatically | 4/5   |
| **Portfolio Intelligence** | Does each opportunity improve future opportunity evaluation?                       | Isolated records | Institutional memory compounds        | 5/5   |
| **Domain Context**         | Does experience in one business area improve guidance elsewhere?                   | Siloed reviews   | Cross-functional learning             | 4/5   |
| **Network**                | Does each new team increase organizational knowledge and governance effectiveness? | Isolated teams   | Enterprise-wide learning system       | 3/5   |

### Correction Loop – 4/5

**What you capture today:**
Every opportunity passes through classification, responsibility design, validation, and governance review. Accepted, revised, and rejected recommendations create structured feedback.

**How it compounds:**
Over time the system learns which classifications, autonomy recommendations, and governance paths were accepted, rejected, or required revision. Future opportunities start from patterns proven successful in previous reviews.

---

### Portfolio Intelligence Loop – 5/5

**What you capture today:**
Every opportunity record, decision, condition, pilot outcome, and governance rationale becomes part of a centralized portfolio.

**How it compounds:**
The system develops organizational memory. Teams can identify recurring use cases, common risk patterns, successful pilot characteristics, and previously approved governance models. Each new opportunity strengthens future evaluation quality.

---

### Domain Context Loop – 4/5

**What you capture today:**
The system captures business function, work type, risk level, autonomy recommendations, accountability structures, and governance outcomes across multiple domains.

**How it compounds:**
Patterns discovered in Finance, HR, Customer Service, Product, or Supply Chain can inform future evaluations elsewhere. The system becomes increasingly effective at identifying risk profiles and implementation approaches across the enterprise.

---

### Network Loop – 3/5

**What you capture today:**
Multiple teams contribute opportunities, reviews, approvals, comments, and pilot outcomes.

**How it compounds:**
As adoption expands, governance decisions become more standardized and discoverable. However, value primarily accrues within a single organization rather than across customers, limiting traditional network effects.

---

**Total Flywheel Score: 16/20**

**Weakest Loop:** Network Loop

**Fix for weakest loop:** Build cross-team discovery, reusable governance patterns, approved use-case libraries, and recommended responsibility templates that make every team's work more valuable to others.

# Encroachment Threat Assessment

### 1. Platform Encroachment

**Attacker:** Microsoft Copilot / ServiceNow / Atlassian

**Vector:** Add AI governance workflows directly into existing enterprise platforms.

**Time-to-threat:** 12–24 months

**% of value at risk:** 60%

**Why:** Most enterprises already use these systems for workflow management. If governance becomes a feature rather than a product category, platform vendors have distribution advantages.

---

### 2. Vertical Competitor

**Attacker:** Dedicated AI governance vendors

**Vector:** More sophisticated risk assessment, model governance, compliance reporting, and AI inventory management.

**Time-to-threat:** 6–12 months

**% of value at risk:** 40%

**Why:** Governance-focused vendors can move faster and develop deeper expertise in AI oversight.

---

### 3. Adjacent Expansion

**Attacker:** Productboard, Jira, Airtable, Notion

**Vector:** Extend existing intake and portfolio management workflows into AI opportunity governance.

**Time-to-threat:** 18–36 months

**% of value at risk:** 30%

**Why:** Customers may prefer extending familiar tools rather than adopting a separate governance platform.

---

# 90-Day Encroachment Plan

## Attacker

Microsoft Copilot + ServiceNow

## Attack Vector

Target the Portfolio Intelligence Loop by embedding AI opportunity intake, governance workflows, and reporting directly into systems enterprises already use.

### Weeks 1–4: What They Ship

* AI opportunity intake templates
* Governance review workflow
* Risk assessment forms
* Executive reporting dashboards
* Basic approval routing

### Weeks 5–8: How They Poach Users

* Bundle governance capabilities into existing enterprise contracts
* Offer one-click migration from spreadsheets and forms
* Leverage existing security and compliance certifications
* Eliminate procurement friction

### Weeks 9–12: Why Users Don't Come Back

* Existing workflow systems become the default source of truth
* Teams stop maintaining a separate governance platform
* Opportunity history fragments across systems
* Leadership prefers consolidated reporting

### Your Defense

* Become the system of record for AI decision-making, not just workflow management.
* Build proprietary governance intelligence from historical decisions.
* Capture pilot outcomes, autonomy patterns, approval conditions, and accountability structures competitors cannot easily recreate.
* Make portfolio insights and governance recommendations materially better because of accumulated organizational memory.
* Position around "AI operating model intelligence" rather than workflow automation.
